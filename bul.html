The aim: take every one of your opponent's five soldiers captive and march them home to be killed, before they do the same to yours.

A Brief History

Puluc — also called bul or boolik — is a Maya "running-fight" game: two sides advance soldiers head-on along a single narrow lane, capturing not by removing an opponent outright but by taking them prisoner — a captured soldier travels along with its captor until it can be marched home and killed, or rescued by a friendly soldier along the way. The word bul appears across Maya languages simply meaning "to play at dice," and Spanish chroniclers barely mention it — most of what's known comes not from ancient sources but from Mopan and Kekchi Maya communities in Belize and Guatemala, who still play a version of it today. This particular "capture and carry" ruleset was published by the games historian R.C. Bell in 1960, drawing on a 1906 German field account by Karl Sapper; other modern versions exist too, including simpler ones closer to what those living communities actually play today. Whether any of them are unchanged from anything played in antiquity is genuinely unknown — what survives is the word, the dice, and more than one reconstruction of the rules built from it.

How to Play

Bul, using the "capture and carry" reconstruction attributed to R.C. Bell (1960), based on Karl Sapper's 1906 field notes.
  1. The lane. A single row of 9 squares, with a home base for each side at either end. Maize enters from the left and advances rightward; Jaguar enters from the right and advances leftward — straight at each other.
  2. Setup. Each side has 5 soldiers, all waiting in their home base at the start.
  3. Throwing. Each turn you throw four marked corn kernels. Your move is how many land marked-side up — except if all four land blank, which counts as a throw of 5. So every throw gives you 1 to 5.
  4. Entering. Bring a new soldier onto the lane by throwing its distance from your own base.
  5. Moving. Move any soldier you control forward by the number thrown. You can't land on a square held by one of your own free soldiers.
  6. Capturing. Land exactly on an enemy soldier and you take it captive — it isn't removed, just held. From then on it travels along with your soldier as a single stack, wherever it goes.
  7. Rescuing. If your soldier is being held captive, you can free it — land one of your own free soldiers on that stack. Doing so captures the enemy soldier controlling it, and your piece is released to travel with your new soldier instead.
  8. Reaching the far end. If a throw would carry your soldier off the far end of the board, it doesn't go past — it heads straight home to your base instead, along with any of your own soldiers travelling with it. If it was holding any captives at that moment, those captives are killed the instant it arrives.
  9. Passing. If a throw leaves you with no legal move at all, your turn simply passes — no penalty.
  10. Winning. You win the moment every one of your opponent's 5 soldiers is either killed or currently held captive.
